    <h2>Questions people ask before they convert</h2>
    <details open><summary>Will the WAV sound better than the MP3?</summary><p>No, and be suspicious of anything that says yes. The songs we measured arrive as AAC audio at 48 kHz, around 194 kbps. Writing that to WAV changes the container, not the content. The WAV is the better file to <b>edit</b>; it isn't a better recording.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Is it lossless?</summary><p>The WAV container and its PCM samples are lossless — nothing is thrown away after this step. What happened before it was decided upstream, and no converter can undo that. That's why we publish the source bitrate instead of a promise.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Why 48 kHz and not 44.1?</summary><p>Because that's what the source carries. We write at the source rate rather than resampling it to fit a preset.</p></details>
    <details><summary>Does this get around the Pro requirement?</summary><p>It doesn't touch your account at all. We never sign in, never call the download menu, and never change what your plan includes. If you need the files Suno holds for subscribers, that's a question for Suno.</p></details>
    <details><summary>How many can I convert at once?</summary><p>One at a time by default. WAV is about five times heavier than the delivered file, so a large browser-side archive collapses — it fails late and quietly. For a whole set, use the <a href="/suno-bulk-download/">bulk export page</a>, which handles the queue and tells you where the real limit is.</p></details>
